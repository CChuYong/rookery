## rookery v0.4.4

v0.4.4 is about getting a stuck worker back under control: you can now recover a wedged worker in place instead of restarting the daemon, a worker can no longer get stuck spinning `running` after a Stop, and the teardown actions spell out exactly what they do.

_8 commits since v0.4.3._

### 🛟 Recover a wedged worker in place

A worker whose turn is wedged inside a long tool call or a Dynamic Workflow couldn't be stopped by the chat composer's Stop — that's a *soft interrupt*, and it can't break a turn the provider keeps alive. The worker would spin `running` forever, and the only escape was restarting the whole daemon.

- **Stop now escalates to Recover.** If Stop doesn't settle within a few seconds and the worker is still busy, the button turns into a **Recover** action that hard-kills the stuck turn and resumes the *same* session in place — the worker returns to idle with its conversation and worktree intact.
- The in-progress turn's live output is lost (the same tradeoff as a daemon restart), but the worker stays usable — no full restart needed.

### 🔁 No more workers stuck `running`

- **Fixed a worker getting stuck `running` after a Stop.** After interrupting a turn, a stray provider frame could flip the worker back to `running` with no turn ever following it, leaving it spinning indefinitely — and a second Stop couldn't help. A **wake-watchdog** now settles such a fizzled wake back to idle. (Changing a worker's model may briefly flash `running` for a few seconds before it settles — harmless.)
- **A consecutive-error circuit breaker** stops a worker that ends several turns in a row without completing, instead of letting it burn turns in a loop.

### 🏷️ Legible teardown actions

- **One "destroy" action.** `discard` is unified with delete — the master's `discard_worker` tool and the `fleet.discard` path now remove the worker *entirely* (worktree, branch, and record) instead of leaving behind a worktree-less zombie row.
- **The fleet tree's terminal "Stop" is now "End".** It no longer collides with the composer's soft "Stop" (which interrupts just the current turn, keeping the worker). Each teardown action now shows a one-line consequence: **End** keeps the worktree (resumable on restart); **Delete** removes the worktree and uncommitted work (irreversible). The composer Stop gained a "this turn only" tooltip.
